# Living in Altos and Areguá: Rentals, Starting a Business in the Interior and the Cost of Living on €1,000

Paraguay is experiencing a wave of immigration from European and Latin American expats looking for a quieter lifestyle, security and a reduced tax burden. Although most settle in the premium neighborhoods of Asunción, the interior of the country, especially the picturesque towns around the legendary Lake Ypacaraí, such as **Areguá** and **Altos**, offers highly attractive alternatives.

In this guide, based on real testimonies from expats settled in the Cordillera area, we look at the cost of living outside the capital, opportunities for entrepreneurs, the traps of the real estate market and practical tips to optimize your daily finances.

---

## 1. Areguá: Colonial Charm and Outdoor Living

Areguá, in the Central department, is famous for its colonial architecture, its pottery crafts and its strawberry plantations. Set on a hill with spectacular views over Lake Ypacaraí, it is an ideal option for those who want a relaxed pace of life with quick access to Asunción (about 28 km away).

### Costs and Services in Areguá:
* **Living downtown:** Renting a typical house near the historic church puts you steps from restaurants, grocery stores and the local police station.
* **Café prices:** Contrary to what you might think, prices at modern cafés in Areguá are not very different from Asunción. A café con leche (coffee with milk) runs around **25,000 PYG** (~3.40 USD) and a Cold Brew filtered for about 15 hours costs around **30,000 PYG** (~4.10 USD).
* **Access to the beach and lakefront promenade (costanera):** Pedestrian entry to the costanera has a symbolic cost of **1,000 PYG** (~0.14 USD) and parking your car costs **5,000 PYG** (~0.68 USD).
* **Pets and loose dogs:** If you plan to move with your pet, you should know that in Areguá (as in many towns in the interior) there are a lot of community dogs roaming loose in the streets, which calls for caution when walking.

## 2. The Myth of Real Estate Appreciation in Paraguay

A common mistake among new investors is buying property on the promise of quick revaluation, or **capital appreciation**. 

Expats with years of experience in the country warn that **the short-term resale market in Paraguay is not dynamic**:
1. **Oversupply of new properties:** In Asunción and surrounding areas there is a constant construction boom. It is very hard to sell a used apartment when buyers prefer to purchase new units directly from developers, who also offer generous installment plans.
2. **Abundant land:** The same happens with lots in gated communities. If you buy a lot to speculate and resell it in a year, you will be competing with the developer itself, which keeps selling plots with easy payment terms. 
3. **Recommendation:** Buying land or property in Paraguay should focus on building for your own use or long-term rental, not on short-term capital speculation.

## 3. Living in Altos: Is It Possible on €1,000 a Month?

Altos, in the Cordillera department about 50 km from Asunción, is one of the oldest towns in the country. With a cool climate and surrounded by hills, it has become a magnet for expats seeking absolute tranquility.

### Roberto's Experience (an Andalusian around the world):
Roberto, a Spanish expat from Córdoba who has lived in Paraguay for 6 years, shares his experience living in Altos:
* **Extremely cheap rents:** While premium rents in Asunción exceed 700 USD, in Altos rents for comfortable houses or apartments range from **1,500,000 to 2,500,000 PYG** per month (approximately **200 to 340 USD**).
* **The €1,000 budget:** With a monthly income of €1,000 (about 8 million guaraníes), a single person or a couple can live comfortably in Altos, covering rent, food, fuel and leisure without restrictions.
* **Inexpensive telecommunications:** A complete fiber-optic internet package with digital TV (including local football, the Champions League and international channels) costs about **180,000 PYG** (~25 USD). Adding two mobile lines, the total spend does not exceed **50 USD** per month.
* **Distance to Asunción:** Altos is about 50 km from the capital. The drive takes roughly 1 to 1.5 hours. It is vital to avoid traveling toward Asunción at 6:00 PM, as traffic can triple the travel time to almost 3 hours.

## 4. Starting a Business in the Interior: Advantages and Opportunities

Paraguay is an extremely friendly country for the self-employed and the small entrepreneur. Roberto founded a parcel and courier company in Altos that connects the Cordillera department with Central, taking advantage of the lack of formal local logistics services.

### Advantages of the Tax System for Entrepreneurs:
* **No self-employment fees:** Unlike Spain and other European countries, Paraguay has no mandatory fixed monthly fee for being self-employed. If you don't invoice, you don't pay.
* **Very low taxes:** The flat **10-10-10** tax system (10% VAT, 10% Corporate Income Tax, or IRE) makes keeping a company extremely inexpensive. The municipal opening license (patente) is paid only once a year and costs next to nothing.
* **Fast paperwork:** Once you have your Paraguayan Cédula de Identidad (national ID card), getting your Registro Único de Contribuyentes (RUC, taxpayer ID) from the SET (tax authority) takes only **4 to 5 days**.
* **Niche in the interior:** The interior lacks many specialized and formal services. Neighboring towns such as **Atyrá** (famous for its leather crafts such as thermos covers and guampas) constantly demand efficient distribution and logistics channels to the capital.

## 5. Key Tips to Optimize Your Money in Paraguay

### Bank Cashbacks (The Residents' Secret):
One of the biggest advantages of being banked in Paraguay is credit card promotions. Unlike Europe, where discounts are 2% or 3%, Paraguayan banks routinely offer **20% to 30% direct cashback** on purchases at supermarkets, restaurants and gas stations on specific days of the week. 

Taking advantage of these promotions with cards from different banks lets you save hundreds of thousands of guaraníes a month on fuel and food.

### Fuel and Practical Services:
* The cost of mid-grade 93/95 octane gasoline is around **1 EUR per liter** (~7,800 PYG), noticeably cheaper than in Europe.
* Unlike in other countries, Paraguayan gas stations have attendants (playeros) who fill the tank for you, clean the windshield and take payment without you having to get out of your car.
* For late-night convenience shopping, the **Biggie** minimarket chain has branches open **24 hours** across almost the entire country, including parts of the interior.

## Conclusion and Tips for Newcomers

If you are considering moving to Paraguay to start a business or retire:
1. **Get to know the country first:** Take a scouting trip. Paraguay has a very hot summer climate and a very particular culture that does not suit every foreigner.
2. **Focus on your profession:** If you come to start a business, do what you do best and be consistent. The Paraguayan market greatly values formality, punctuality and good customer service. With patience and well-done work, financial success is highly achievable in this country of opportunities.
