# Doing Business in Paraguay: Opportunities, the Maquila Law and the Jack Freeman Case

Over the last decade, Paraguay has established itself as one of the most attractive destinations in Latin America for entrepreneurs, investors and technology professionals. With a stable economy, low inflation and an extremely friendly tax system, the country offers a blank canvas for those who know how to spot opportunities.

To understand the real potential of this market, we look at the career of **Jack Freeman**, a prominent Peruvian businessman who arrived in Paraguay in 1989 intending to stay only one year and today, more than 35 years later, has founded multiple successful companies in technology, digital ticketing, real estate development and private custody services in Asunción.

---

## 1. The Paraguayan Tax System: The 10-10-10 Rule

One of the main incentives for basing your company or professional activity in Paraguay is its simple, flat and non-confiscatory tax system. Unlike the complex tax structures of Europe or North America, Paraguay follows the so-called **10-10-10 rule**:

* **10%** Value Added Tax (IVA).
* **10%** Personal Income Tax (IRP) for locally sourced income.
* **10%** Corporate Income Tax (IRE).

### Territoriality and Full Deductibility
Paraguay also applies the **territoriality principle**: income you generate outside the country (for example, exporting software services, international consulting or trading) is **exempt from taxes**.

For expenses incurred locally, the system offers an advantage unheard of in most OECD countries: **almost full deductibility of personal expenses**. As a self-employed professional, you can deduct your supermarket purchases, vehicles, your children's education and services as long as they are properly backed by tax-valid invoices (today 100% electronic). Even purchases of digital services from abroad (such as Amazon or software subscriptions) count as tax credits.

## 2. The Maquila Law: The Gateway to Mercosur

If you are a manufacturer or distributor, Paraguay offers an unparalleled strategic advantage through the **Maquila Law**. The country acts as a tariff-free export platform to the Mercosur giants, mainly Brazil and Argentina.

```
Imported Raw Materials ➔ Assembly in Paraguay (Maquila) ➔ Tariff-free export to Mercosur
```

### How Does the Maquila Regime Work?
1. **Tax-Suspended Imports:** You can import raw materials, machinery and tools into the country without paying tariffs under the suspensive regime.
2. **Local Added Value:** By incorporating a percentage of local value (labor, packaging, boxes or assembly services of between 40% and 50% depending on the sector), the product obtains the **Mercosur Certificate of Origin**.
3. **Single 1% Rate:** Instead of the standard corporate tax, the maquiladora pays a **single tax of 1% on the value of the export invoice**.
4. **Access to Protected Markets:** The product enters Brazil or Argentina without import tariffs, avoiding the heavy trade barriers of those countries (where tariffs on imports from outside the bloc can exceed 200%).

Numerous Asian multinationals (for example, Korean auto parts makers) currently operate under this law in eastern Paraguay to supply Brazilian automotive assembly plants directly.

## 3. The Labor Market: A Shortage of Middle Managers and Executives

A key challenge when founding a company in Paraguay is hiring qualified staff. According to Jack Freeman, there is a significant **shortage of trained middle managers and executives** in finance, advanced management and technology.

This represents an extraordinary opportunity for expats and qualified professionals:
* **Importing Talent:** It is very common for Paraguayan companies to import directors and general managers from neighboring countries such as Argentina.
* **High Net Salaries:** Since the supply of specialized professionals is low, skilled labor in Paraguay is **very well paid**. Combined with a reasonable cost of living and a flat 10% IRP, the real net salary a professional keeps is extremely high compared with Western standards.

## 4. Business Culture: Relationships, Trust and Tereré

If you plan to do business in Paraguay, you must understand that personal relationships are the fundamental pillar of any commercial agreement. Paraguay is known among expats as *"the country of friends"*.

* **The Social Bond Comes First:** Unlike the cold corporate cultures of Europe or the United States, where business is strictly transactional, in Paraguay protocol demands socializing. You drink tereré (cold yerba mate infusion) and talk about family, clubs and sports before discussing numbers.
* **The Name on the Door:** Historically, many successful companies in Paraguay carry their founder's surname (for example, *J. Fleisman & Cía.*). This is because local businesspeople and consumers value **knowing exactly who they are doing business with**. Putting your own name on the storefront is synonymous with transparency, guarantee and commitment.
* **Finding a Local Partner or Advisor:** To ease the "newcomer's dues" (derecho de piso), it is highly recommended to have a local partner or established resident consultants who help you understand the pace of the market and supplier timelines, and who introduce you to networking circles.

## 5. Quality of Life and Safety in Asunción

One of the factors that most surprises investors who settle in Paraguay is the social peace and everyday safety, especially in the capital.

Jack Freeman points out that, after 35 years living in Asunción and driving high-end vehicles, he has never feared driving late at night or suffering crime out of envy or social resentment:

> *"Asunción is one of the few capitals where humble houses stand next to mansions without any conflict or resentment. I can get a flat tire at night in any neighborhood of Asunción and change it calmly, certain that the neighbors will come over to help me rather than harm me."*

This tranquility, together with proximity to hubs such as São Paulo and Buenos Aires (under two hours by direct flight) and daily flights to Madrid, makes Paraguay an unrivaled base for both business and life in the Southern Cone.
