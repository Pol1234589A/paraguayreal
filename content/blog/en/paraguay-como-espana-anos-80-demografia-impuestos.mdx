# Why Does Paraguay Feel Like 1980s Spain? Demographics, Taxes and Growth

Many middle-aged Spaniards who visit or settle in Paraguay immediately come out with the same phrase: *"This is like living in the Spain of the 80s."* Far from being mere nostalgia for the *movida* or the music of Mecano, this comparison rests on very precise socioeconomic and demographic realities.

In this analysis we compare the clearest parallels between Paraguay in 2026 and Spain in full bloom after the democratic transition, and how you can position yourself to take advantage of these historic opportunities.

---

## 1. Demographics: A Young and Productive Population Pyramid

The fundamental key to any thriving economy is its demographics.

* **Spain in the 80s:** After the end of Franco's dictatorship (1975), the large baby boomer generation born between the 1950s and 1970s entered the labor market. The median age in Spain was barely 29. It was a country of motivated young workers, with a wide pyramid base and very few retirees depending on the state.
* **Paraguay today:** After consolidating its own democracy (Stroessner's dictatorship fell in 1989), the country has a median age of just **26 years**. **70% of Paraguay's population is under 40**. Paraguay's population pyramid has the same triangular shape that Spain's had 40 years ago: an immense productive mass and very few pensioners.
* **The European contrast:** While population aging in Europe has inverted the pyramid, compromising the solvency of pensions and driving up public spending, Paraguay enjoys a spectacular demographic dividend that ensures a labor force and solvency for the coming decades.

---

## 2. Investment Grade and Real Estate Growth

Spain's entry into the European Union in 1986 stamped it with an international seal of security that unleashed a wave of foreign investment and an unprecedented real estate boom. Smart people bought plots and apartments for "next to nothing" ("cuatro duros") in strategic areas of Madrid (such as the Paseo de la Castellana) or Barcelona, multiplying their value decades later.

In Paraguay we are witnessing an identical phenomenon:
* **Investment Grade:** The recent attainment of **Investment Grade** from international agencies such as Moody's acts as the equivalent of Spain's EU accession, attracting international funds and massive foreign capital by guaranteeing the country's macroeconomic and legal stability.
* **Affordable prices:** With average prices per square meter starting at **1,300 USD** in central areas and modern residential neighborhoods, access to private property is highly feasible, something unthinkable today in Spain, where high inflation and bank restrictions force young people to set aside more than 30% of their income just for a mortgage down payment.

---

## 3. The 10-10-10 Rule vs. the European Tax Squeeze

Paraguay's appeal for the self-employed, digital nomads and entrepreneurs lies not only in its low costs, but in the simplicity and lightness of its tax system.

| Tax | Spain Today | Paraguay Today |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Value Added Tax (VAT/IVA)** | 21% | 10% |
| **Personal Income Tax (IRPF)** | Up to 47% | 10% (on a territorial basis) |
| **Corporate Income Tax (IRE)** | 25% | 10% |
| **Monthly Self-Employed Contribution** | Mandatory (high, variable) | **Does not exist** (you only pay if you invoice) |
| **Public Debt / GDP** | 105% | **36.7%** |

This minimal tax burden allows for high solvency and leaves real room for private capital to keep investing and creating jobs, just as happened in Spain in the 70s and 80s, before public spending and state debt skyrocketed.

---

## 4. 100% Clean Energy and Geopolitical Independence

While Spain was hit hard by the oil crises of 1973 and 1979 because of its absolute dependence on foreign crude (which triggered inflation above 14%), Paraguay built, during those same decades, the foundations of its energy independence: the gigantic binational dams of **Itaipú** (with Brazil) and **Yacyretá** (with Argentina).

* **Hydroelectric superpower:** Paraguay produces 100% clean, renewable and cheap energy, consuming only 15% of its share and exporting the rest to its neighbors.
* **Attracting technology:** This cheap electricity surplus is massively attracting data centers, crypto-asset mining farms and textile factories.
* **Electric mobility:** With global fuel prices rising, getting around in hybrid or electric cars is increasingly economical in Paraguay, with laughably low home charging costs.

---

## 5. Continental Connectivity Megaprojects

Just as Spain built its public and private fortune by laying networks of highways and railways in the 80s, Paraguay is now carrying out two key projects that will change its geographic role on the continent:

1. **The Bioceanic Route (Ruta Bioceánica):** A mega-highway crossing the entire Paraguayan Chaco, linking the Atlantic Ocean (Brazil) with the Pacific (Chile). This corridor will act as a "land-based Panama Canal" for international trade, and the investors who are buying land at low prices along its margins today are securing enormous future appreciation.
2. **The Commuter Train (Tren de Cercanías):** A project financed in partnership with the United Arab Emirates that will create the first commuter rail line connecting the capital, Asunción, with Lake Ypacaraí, boosting logistics and property values in the intermediate cities.
