# 24/7 Services and Quality of Life in Paraguay: What Surprises Foreigners

When a foreigner, particularly a European or North American, plans a move to Paraguay, they are usually drawn by the low taxes and the subtropical climate. However, once settled in the country, they discover a series of everyday services and quality-of-life aspects that surpass many developed countries and leave them open-mouthed.

From the extreme convenience of its shops to the unhurried rhythm of day-to-day life, in this guide we look at 24/7 services, local consumer culture and practical tips for newcomers.

---

## 1. Consumer Convenience: 24-Hour Banks, Biggie and Service Stations

Paraguay's service sector stands out for a customer-oriented convenience that is extremely hard to find in Europe today.

* **Banks with 24-hour in-person service:** In Paraguay there are banks (such as Sudameris and others) with premium branches that offer in-person service with real agents **24 hours a day, 365 days a year**. If you lose your card or have a financial emergency at 3:00 AM on a Sunday, you can go to a modern branch, have a complimentary coffee and be served face to face by an officer, and they even have playrooms for children while you take care of your paperwork.
* **Biggie stores, open 24/7:** Forget about being left without supplies on Sundays or at night. **Biggie** stores are a chain of mini-markets open 24/7 found on almost every corner of the cities. They are not mere kiosks: they sell everything, from fresh food, dairy and packaged meats to charcoal, ice and alcoholic beverages. They have also recently launched the **Biggie Pharma** division, offering access to 24-hour pharmacies.
* **High-end service station markets:** The convenience stores at service stations (such as Petrobras, Copetrol, Shell) compete fiercely with one another in design, architecture and food offerings. Far from being quick, grubby stops, they are true specialty cafes and designer venues where Paraguayans meet to work, have breakfast or dine. The service is also full: the station attendant ("playero") fills the tank for you without you having to get out of the vehicle.

## 2. Slow Life: Lowering Stress Levels

Many expatriates who move to Asunción from large European capitals such as Barcelona or Madrid experience a notable drop in their stress levels thanks to the **slow life** culture:

* **An unhurried pace:** People live in a calmer, more relaxed way. Although work responsibilities remain the same, you don't feel the constant rush or nervous hurry of the cities of the northern hemisphere.
* **The day stretches further:** This is a unanimous comment among newcomers. With no tourist overcrowding and no endless lines in shopping centers, supermarkets or fitting rooms, errands and shopping are done quickly and pleasantly, freeing up time to enjoy social or family life.
* **Urban nature:** Cities like Asunción stand out for their immense mass of trees and urban green areas, integrating jungle and nature into everyday life.

## 3. Financial Solutions for Newcomers

Opening a traditional bank account in Paraguay can be a bureaucratic process that takes weeks or months while you wait to obtain your Paraguayan Cédula de Identidad (national ID card).

* **The Meru solution:** Many expatriates handle their initial fund transfers through financial apps such as **Meru**.
* **How it works:** The app lets you transfer your home-country euros or dollars with minimal fees and excellent exchange rates directly to local Paraguayan accounts in guaraníes or dollars (for example, using the account of a partner, a trusted relative or your own account once approved). Meru operates in more than 180 countries and is an indispensable tool for the transition period of any digital nomad or immigrant.

## 4. The Subtropical Winter: A Necessary Warning

Although Paraguay is sold as a country of constant heat and scorching sun (with summers above 40°C), the reality is that the Paraguayan winter (especially in June and July) can bring surprises:

* **Cold snaps:** It is common for the country to be hit by polar air masses from Patagonia, pushing temperatures down to a range of **2°C to 5°C** for weeks.
* **Lack of preparation in homes:** Since most Paraguayan apartments and houses are designed strictly to ventilate and fight summer heat (high ceilings, no thermal insulation or central heating), cold winter days can feel very intense indoors. It is advisable to bring suitable warm clothing and make sure the installed air conditioners have a heating function (*cold/heat split units*).
