# Paperwork with Just a Passport in Paraguay: Bank Accounts, Buying Cars and the Real Cost of Living

One of the most frequent questions among foreigners planning a move to Paraguay is whether they need the Paraguayan Cédula de Identidad (national ID card) to carry out everyday transactions. There is a myth that without approved residency and the cédula in hand, the expatriate has their hands tied.

The reality is very different. In this guide, based on testimonies from European expatriates living in the south of the country, we look at **which important procedures you can do with only your passport**, the infrastructure differences between cities and the real cost of basic utilities.

---

## 1. Opening a Bank Account with Just a Passport

Many foreigners assume that to open a bank account and get banked locally, the Paraguayan cédula is mandatory. However, it is perfectly feasible to open an account using only your valid passport.

* **The case of Banco Sudameris:** Institutions such as **Banco Sudameris** (particularly at branches in the interior such as Encarnación) allow foreigners to start opening savings or checking accounts by presenting their passport.
* **Advantage for newcomers:** This lets you operate commercially, receive international transfers and manage your local expenses from the first weeks after your arrival, while your residency application follows its legal course before Migraciones (the immigration authority).

---

## 2. Buying a Brand-New Car with a Passport or National ID

If you need immediate mobility when you arrive in the country, you can buy a vehicle in your name without major bureaucratic complications:

* **Purchase and transfer:** Paraguayan dealerships and *escribanías* (notary offices) allow the purchase, invoicing and transfer of new or used vehicles using your **passport** or your home-country ID document (such as the Argentine DNI).
* **Warning for border crossings:** If you buy a vehicle with your passport or foreign national ID and register your legal address in your home country, you could face customs obstacles if you try to cross the border in that vehicle into Argentina or Brazil. To move freely across borders with a Paraguayan license plate, it is advisable to wait until you have the Paraguayan civil identity card (cédula).

## 3. The Real Cost of Electricity: Summer vs. Winter

Electricity supply in Paraguay is provided as a monopoly by ANDE (Administración Nacional de Electricidad, the National Electricity Administration). Although rates are extremely cheap, consumption varies strongly with the climate:

* **Winter (no air conditioning):** During the cool months, the electricity spending of an average apartment is minimal, ranging between **50,000 and 60,000 PYG** per month (approximately **$7 to $8 USD**).
* **Summer (air conditioning 24/7):** In summer, because of the intense subtropical heat, average bills rise to a range of **250,000 to 300,000 PYG** per month (around **$35 to $40 USD**).
* **The golden tip:** Local residents recommend **never setting up automatic payment of the ANDE bill** on your card. It is better to check it manually each month before paying it, to catch possible overbilling or erroneous meter readings before the money is debited.

## 4. Retiring in Paraguay: Can You Live on €1,000 a Month?

The short answer is **yes, absolutely**. With a pension or passive income of €1,000 a month, a person or couple can live comfortably in the country, especially if they choose cities outside the capital's corporate hub.

* **Recommended alternative cities:** Places such as **Altos (Cordillera)**, near Lake Ypacaraí, or **Encarnación (Itapúa)** offer much more affordable rents than Asunción and an extremely peaceful pace of life.
* **Premium infrastructure in the south:** Cities such as **Encarnación** stand out for impeccable urban infrastructure, wide and well-kept sidewalks, streets without potholes and a clean riverfront promenade (Playa San José) free of annoying noise, surpassing many areas of the capital in order and maintenance.

## 5. The Cédula Delay: The Procedure Between Governments

A common complaint among new residents is the delay in issuing the Paraguayan Cédula de Identidad after obtaining the temporary residency card.

This delay is usually not due to Paraguayan bureaucracy, but to an international verification step:
1. When you apply for the cédula, Paraguay's Identification Department requests validation of your **criminal record directly from the government of your home country** (such as Spain).
2. This exchange of security information between foreign ministries and justice ministries can take **between 3 and 4 months**, during which time your cédula application remains waiting for an official response from abroad.

## 6. Healthcare and the Cheap-Electricity Myth

It is often debated whether Paraguay's low electricity costs and taxes end up being offset by paying for private health insurance ("mutuas") or by deficient infrastructure.

European expatriates clarify that this dilemma is false: in their home countries (such as Spain) they were already paying for private health insurance to avoid the long waiting times and saturation of the public health system. Therefore, taking out private medical coverage in Paraguay does not represent a real additional cost, but rather the maintenance of their usual health standard in high-quality private clinics.
