# Tourist Guide to Asunción: What to See in the Historic Center and Travel Tips

Asunción, founded in 1537 as the "Mother of Cities," is one of the oldest capitals in South America. Despite its rich history, its bilingual Spanish-Guaraní culture and its vibrant atmosphere, it remains one of the least-visited destinations on the continent. This is a great advantage for independent travelers looking to discover an authentic destination, free of tourist crowds and full of hospitality.

In this guide we take you on a complete tour of its historic center and main attractions, and give you the essential practical tips on transport, food and money transactions.

---

## 1. What to See in Downtown Asunción: The Essential Route

### The Costanera of Asunción:
This is a beautiful, wide promenade 7 to 8 kilometers long that runs along the bay of the Paraguay River. Built a little over a decade ago, it is the locals' favorite place to go running, ride a bike, stroll and enjoy the sunset with a good **tereré**. It has a small river beach and a very relaxed atmosphere.

### Palacio de los López (Government Palace):
Located a few steps from the Costanera, this impressive neoclassical building is the official seat of Paraguay's presidency. Surrounded by gardens and amiably guarded, it is one of the most photogenic monuments in the city. Behind it is the **Plaza de los Desaparecidos** (Plaza of the Disappeared), a space of remembrance for the victims of the military dictatorship that ruled the country for 35 years (1954-1989).

### Panteón Nacional de los Héroes (National Pantheon of Heroes):
This mausoleum and architectural gem of Napoleonic inspiration in the heart of the historic center holds the remains of the national heroes and military men who forged the country. Its design is circular and makes visitors lean over the upper railing to see the tombs located below ground, naturally paying their respects with this physical gesture.

### Plaza Uruguaya:
A leafy park that serves as a green lung amid the downtown bustle. Its shaded paths are the perfect place to buy traditional books, try street food and see the traditional stalls selling *yuyos* (medicinal herbs) and tereré.

## 2. Street Food: Tereré and Sopa Paraguaya

Discovering Asunción is also an extremely original culinary experience. Be sure to try these two local specialties on your tour:

* **Tereré:** This is the official drink and an intangible cultural heritage of humanity according to UNESCO. It is the cold version of mate, served in a *guampa* (drinking horn or cup) with plenty of ice and water mixed with freshly crushed *yuyos* (such as mint, boldo or lemon verbena). Unlike in Argentina, in Paraguay it is **taken strictly with water and herbs, never with artificial juices**.
* **Sopa Paraguaya:** Although its name suggests a liquid dish, sopa paraguaya is actually a **dry soup**. It is a tasty savory cake made from cornmeal, local cheese (queso Paraguay), onions, milk and eggs. It was born from a mistake by the cook of President Carlos Antonio López, who let the corn broth evaporate too much and ended up serving it as a solid that delighted the president.

## 3. Practical Tips for Getting Around and Surviving

### Public and Private Transport (Uber and Bolt):
* **Uber/Bolt:** Getting around Asunción with ride-hailing apps is extremely cheap and safe. A typical trip of up to 10 minutes costs just **1.50 to 2.00 USD** (11,000 to 15,000 PYG), while longer 20-minute rides or trips to the airport rarely exceed **7.00 or 8.00 USD** (50,000 to 60,000 PYG). The **Bolt** app is usually even cheaper and widely used compared to Uber.
* **Colectivos:** City buses are quite old, deficient and sometimes unsafe because of the drivers' reckless driving. It is advisable to avoid them if you have the budget for Uber or Bolt.

### The ATM Trap:
If you travel with foreign cards (credit or debit), you need to be very careful with cash withdrawals:
1. **Very low withdrawal limit:** The vast majority of ATMs in Paraguay limit withdrawals with international cards to just **100,000 or 150,000 PYG** per transaction (about 13 to 20 USD).
2. **Very high fees:** Each transaction carries a local bank fee of approximately **3 USD** (25,000 PYG).
3. **Recommendation:** Avoid repeatedly withdrawing small amounts from the ATM, since you will end up paying between 15% and 20% in fees. The best approach is to bring dollars or euros in cash from your home country and exchange them at physical exchange houses (such as Cambios Chaco or Cambios Guaraní) or pay by card directly at established businesses.
